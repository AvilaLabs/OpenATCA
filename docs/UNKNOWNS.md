# Open unknowns

Every entry says why it is unknown and what settles it.

| ID | Area | Unknown | Why | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UK-001 | Boundaries | Whether NASR ARTCC boundary points are joined by geodesics or rhumb lines | No FAA document found states it; JO 7900.1F says only "straight lines" | Model stores both and reports the separation (OA-ASP-005); ask FAA AIS during outreach |
| UK-002 | CIFP | Fields parsed but not verified against a second source: header CRC; ER col 44; PD/PE/PF cols 26, 45-47 (RNP), 117, 119, 120; localizer cols 75-83; PG cols 91-95; PA cols 62-70, 82-85; RF arc radius scale; southern-hemisphere MORA convention; H/J/V/X/Y and other altitude-description codes outside the narrow approach cases below; TCH indicator values D/R; UR continuation types other than C | [Navigraph DFD v1](https://developers.navigraph.com/docs/navigation-data/dfd-data-format) states that it uses ARINC 424-18 and documents blank/@ Altitude 1 plus H/J field meanings (Introduction; Appendix “Altitude Description”); ARINC-authored [424-17 §5.29](https://s8f1feefd4f70cc50.jimcontent.com/download/version/1347002662/module/6126894715/name/ARINC%20424-17.pdf) independently documents blank Altitude 1 and J. This verifies only those field mappings, not every CIFP rule or KORD procedure applicability. The resolver currently accepts blank descriptor with numeric Altitude 1 and no Altitude 2 as an exact crossing; for approach legs only, H/J retain numeric Altitude 1 as an at-or-above crossing floor and Altitude 2 as distinct glide-slope metadata. It does not synthesize the glide-slope path. V and blank Altitude-2-only remain refused; chart/profile-specific applicability is not inferred. | Verify remaining fields against CIFP Readme exceptions and charted values before later use. Keep unsupported descriptor/value shapes refused; independently verify procedure applicability before expanding the supported subset. |
| UK-003 | Procedures | Radar-vector SIDs (for example O'HARE NINE at KORD, computer code NOT ASSIGNED in NASR) exist in NASR but are not coded in CIFP | CIFP codes only procedures with a defined path | Model takes such procedures from NASR, marked as having no coded legs |
| UK-004 | SWIM latency | How late SCDS data arrives compared with when the source produced it. Measured 2026-11-14 on one 60 s sample per feed, with the receiver clock NTP-synchronised: SFDPS arrives a steady 58–61 s after each `flight` element's `timestamp`; TBFM 7–9 s after `envTime`; TFMS 1–8 s after `sourceTimeStamp`, which has whole-second resolution | It is not known whether SCDS deliberately delays public distribution, or whether each timestamp marks generation, last update or something else. A backlog draining would make the lag shrink over the run; it stayed steady | Measure continuously with `oa-feed`'s latency histogram over hours, and confirm the field meanings and any distribution delay from FAA SCDS and SFDPS documentation. Until settled, no function may treat SCDS data as current to better than the measured lag |
| UK-005 | Time and position integrity | Where trustworthy time and position come from when GNSS is jammed or spoofed. ADS-B positions and the receiver clocks that time-stamp them both depend on GPS, and GNSS interference is reported to be increasing | No design yet; radar gives independent position but not independent time, and the comparator assumes a correct clock | Survey FAA and EUROCONTROL GNSS-interference reporting and the time-source backups in use (atomic references, network time, eLoran); define how a facility detects a wrong clock and quarantines GNSS-derived inputs that disagree with radar (principle 2a) |
| UK-006 | Backbone-independent links | Which path, if any, can carry essential data between facilities when the shared network fails (SCOPE principle 8) | Capacity, latency and approval of satellite or HF data links for ATC use not studied | Size the essential data per facility, then survey existing aviation satellite and HF data services |
| UK-007 | Flight data from its source | Where a facility node gets flight plans, amendments and interfacility coordination without depending on another facility's automation; SWIM's SFDPS is ERAM output, so it fails with the centre that produces it (docs/NODE_NETWORK.md) | Public documents (2026-11-19 survey) show every flight-data path ends at one centre's ERAM: filed plans are addressed over NADIN/AFTN to the departure centre (ERAS Guide v3.2 2.1), and controller amendments, assigned altitudes, beacon assignments and handoff state exist only in the controlling centre until forwarded. The hubs (NADIN, the NAM gateway) do not depend on any one centre. In 2014 every neighbour processed Chicago's flight plans by hand for the whole outage (OIG AV-2015-112). No public document describes automatic re-addressing of a failed centre's plans | Read NAS-IC-82424301-01 (NADIN) for re-addressing and JO 7110.10DD for filer fallback; specify node subscription to plans addressed to the centres it backs up, and controller entries as replicated engine inputs once operational |
| UK-008 | Reference independence | How far errors shared between the truth and the tracker's ADS-B input bias the scores. Truth is built from the other half of each aircraft's own reports (M3_DESIGN 1), so it shares that transponder's position error, a wrong reported accuracy (NACp) and its latency. A shared latency or bias favours the system that relies most on ADS-B, which matters for the comparison with ERAM | The report split removes report-level noise dependence only; the per-aircraft, per-system time-offset fit of P-003 absorbs a constant along-track lag but not a lag that varies, a position bias or a misreported accuracy. A simulation measured the size of the blind spot (`crates/oa-track/tests/optimism.rs`, OA-SIM-033 to 049): with 8% of aircraft offset by three times their reported 95% bound and 9% reporting 0.2 to 0.8 s late, the position error at report times against exact truth was 18.6 to 23.7 m over three seeds at 1 s spacing, while the same error scored our way read 2.6 m; with report noise added the figures were 24.5 m and 9.1 m. Between reports at 12 s spacing our way read 88 to 100% of the true error. Errors placed only in the tracker's half were seen in full, so the blind spot is the sharing, not the method. How common such errors are in US traffic is not known: the rates simulated are assumptions. A first screen against ERAM (`oa-shadow screen`, 15 November 2026) found a fleet-wide ADS-B latency of 0.49 s (95% interval 0.41 to 0.56 s) over 91 aircraft, which the time-offset fit absorbs; only 5 aircraft had enough clean samples to be screened one by one, and none of them stood out, so the prevalence is still unknown. Until it is known, a position score at report times is a measure of agreement with ADS-B, not of accuracy | Measure per-transponder offset and lag against ERAM (`oa-shadow screen`) and against terminal radar from STDDS, keeping radar-only tracks apart because STARS also uses ADS-B; score against a reconstructed reference that also uses radar (the open-source Simple Reconstructor of OpenATS COMPASS, run as an outside tool); an independent GPS recording is the only check of the reference itself |
| UK-009 | STDDS reference | Whether a STARS track update flagged without ADS-B (TAIS `adsb` false) has a position free of ADS-B, so that it can serve as a reference independent of ADS-B for UK-008 | The flag is not documented in the public STDDS material. The first census (20 November 2026, `tools/oa-stdds-census`) shows it mostly marks a track with no Mode S identity: 98% of such updates have a zero address, and the flag changes within a track on 1.3% of consecutive updates. That does not show which sensors fed the position | The position comparison of `oa-stdds-census` against the same day's ADS-B, read with its chance-match estimate and coverage counts, by track class: a radar-only position differs from ADS-B by radar error, growing with range; one built with ADS-B looks like adsb=true, which is ADS-B-fused and no independent control (its difference measures STARS latency and filtering). An adsb=false update on a mixed track is not ADS-B-free |
